# M.Zuiko 8mm f/1.8 Fisheye PRO: Aperture & Optical Behavior Guide

When shooting with the **M.Zuiko 8mm f/1.8 Fisheye PRO** behind a curved dome port, changing the aperture alters the entire optical geometry of your shot, not just your exposure. This guide outlines the relationship between aperture, dome port physics (the virtual image concept), and overall image quality in underwater wide-angle photography.

---

## 1. The Core Physics: Dome Ports & The Virtual Image

A curved dome port acts as a supplementary optical element that reshapes light rays underwater. Instead of allowing the lens to see infinity directly, the dome creates a curved **virtual image** that floats just centimeters in front of the dome glass. 

The camera sensor must focus directly on this curved virtual image. Because the virtual image is curved and standard camera sensors are flat, shooting at wide-open apertures creates an inherent geometric mismatch. This physical property is what dictates the sharpness of your corners versus the center of your frame.

## 3. Deep-Dive Field Tactics

### The f/1.8 "Creative Cheat" (Portraits & CFWA)
* **The Optical Behavior:** The center of the lens is spectacularly sharp at f/1.8, but corner softening is unavoidable due to the dome's optical limitations. 
* **Execution:** Turn this limitation into a creative asset. Position your primary subject (e.g., a large dahlia anemone, a jewel anemone cluster, or a curious fish) dead-center in the frame and get as close to the dome glass as your lighting permits. 
* **Framing Warning:** Ensure no critical structural details, dive buddies, or key environmental features sit in the outer 20% of the frame, as they will be subject to stretching and optical softness. Let the corners melt into smooth, ambient green or blue bokeh.

### The f/8.0 "Landscape Insurance Policy" (Structures & Vistas)
* **The Optical Behavior:** Stopping down to f/8.0 sharpens the corners by increasing the depth of field enough to span the entire curvature of the dome's virtual image. It flattens out edge stretching, chromatic aberrations, and corner softness.
* **Execution:** Use this when swimming through dramatic kelp canopies, framing pier pilings covered in life, or tracking reef walls. 
* **Ambient Management:** Because f/8.0 cuts down on the light reaching the sensor, it must be paired with your C2 dial technique: drop your shutter speed to **1/60s, 1/80s, or 1/100s** to open the exposure window and pull a rich, glowing emerald gradient into the background water column.

### The f/11 - f/16 "Sunstar Exception"
* **The Optical Behavior:** Micro Four Thirds sensors hit diffraction limits early. At f/11 and beyond, fine details begin to soften across the entire frame.
* **Execution:** Only run these tight apertures if you are shooting directly up into the sun from a shallow depth to create a crisp, multi-pointed sunstar effect. If your composition does not contain an actual sun ball, stay at f/8.0 or wider to protect fine image contrast.
