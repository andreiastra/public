# Simple Optical Summary: OM-1 Mark II Macro Setup (Air vs. Water)

This document provides a clean, straightforward breakdown of how your OM SYSTEM OM-1 Mark II and M.Zuiko 60mm f/2.8 Macro lens behave inside the Nauticam housing, explaining the physical numbers from your real-world ruler test.

---

## 1. Shooting in Air (Your Ruler Test Explained)
When testing your setup on land with a ruler placed directly against the front of the housing, critical focus peaks at **$7.3 \text{ cm}$** away from the port glass.
* **The Glass Refraction Effect:** The $8 \text{ mm}$ thick optical BK-7 glass window of your port acts as a dense physical layer. Because glass bends light more than air, it creates a minor "virtual image" shift. This tricks the lens into seeing the subject as if it were slightly further away, allowing the entire housing assembly to creep closer to the ruler (dropping from the theoretical unhoused $9.0 \text{ cm}$ clearance down to your real-world $7.3 \text{ cm}$) before hitting the internal physical focus limit.
* **Magnification:** The lens operates at its true, native **$1.0\times$ macro magnification** (1:1 reproduction ratio).

---

## 2. Shooting Underwater (Submerged Dynamics)
When the housing is submerged in water, the light path is split into two distinct mediums: a $10.0 \text{ cm}$ air chamber inside the housing and the water column outside. Light passing through the flat port interface shifts your spatial boundaries and magnification scales.
* **Minimum Working Distance ($WD_{\text{port}}$):** Because water refraction pushes the apparent image forward, you cannot get as close to your subjects underwater. To achieve absolute minimum focus, the subject must be at a physical distance of **$12.0 \text{ cm}$** away from the external front face of the port glass.
* **Sensor-to-Subject Distance ($MFD$):** Your total physical minimum focus distance becomes **$22.0 \text{ cm}$** from the camera sensor plane to the subject ($10.0 \text{ cm}$ internal air gap + $12.0 \text{ cm}$ physical water column).
* **The Flat Port Magnification Boost:** A flat port acts like a mild magnifying glass underwater, compressing your diagonal field of view by roughly $25\%$ to $33\%$. This field reduction gives you a practical, frame-filling magnification of **$1.33\times$** ($2.66\times$ in 35mm full-frame equivalence). 
* **Sensor Coverage:** Because of this crop effect, an ultra-macro subject area measuring just **$9.8 \text{ mm} \times 12.8 \text{ mm}$** will completely fill your frame on the sensor.
