# eOceanic Shore Diving Intelligence & Site Analysis

A detailed analysis of how the 91 coastal locations extracted from [eOceanic](https://eoceanic.com/sailing/harbours/europe/ireland/cork/) ([`eoceanic_cork_sites.json`](eoceanic_cork_sites.json)) inform shore diving, dive planning, underwater photography, and coastal safety across County Cork.

> **Note on Map Links:** Every location name below links directly to its geographic coordinates on Google Maps.

---

## 1. Overview & Data Translation

eOceanic documents coastal harbours, anchorages, and landing havens from a maritime perspective. When decoded for shore diving and underwater photography, its entries provide critical intelligence on **physical access, tidal currents, swell exposure, bottom substrate, reef ecology, and underwater topography**.

### Marine Terminology to Shore Diving Translation

| eOceanic Field / Marine Metric | Boating Meaning | Shore Diving & Photography Translation |
|---|---|---|
| **`shelter_stars` (1 to 5)** | Anchorage protection from wind/sea | Likelihood of calm entry/exit, minimal surge, and workable visibility. |
| **`min_depth_m` (LAT/LWS)** | Sounding depth at lowest tide in anchorage | Baseline water depth immediately off the pier head / slipway base. |
| **`Restriction: shallow/drying pier`** | Vessel cannot berth at low water | Pier/slip dries completely; entries/exits must be timed around High Water to avoid carrying heavy gear over exposed rocks/mud. |
| **`Jetty / Slipway / Landing`** | Quayside infrastructure for boats | Confirmed shore entry/exit points (ramps, steps, ladders, slipways). |
| **`Note: strong tides or currents`** | Navigation hazard under sail/engine | **High risk:** must dive strictly at slack water or avoid spring tide cycles. |
| **`Note: fish farming activity`** | Surface cages & support vessels | **Entanglement hazard:** subsurface mooring cables, cage grids, tow ropes. |
| **`Water available via tap`** | Fresh water replenishment for tanks | Fresh water rinse available on site for cameras, lenses, regulators, and drysuits. |

### Critical Tidal Flow Notes for Divers
* **Cork Harbour Narrows & East Passage ([East Ferry Marina (#7)](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.8661652,-8.2131662), [Glenbrook (#11)](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.8595314,-8.3316832), [Cork Harbour Marina (#12)](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.8446655,-8.3325005)):** Tidal streams reach **3.0 knots at spring tides**. Shore diving near Monkstown, Glenbrook, or East Ferry is only viable at High Water Slack; otherwise, strong currents cause extreme drift and zero visibility.
* [**Knockadoon Sound (#2)**](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.8867683,-7.8658333): Contracted 200m channel where tides run at **2.0 knots** with heavy overfalls over rocky ledges. Must be dived at slack water.
* [**Dursey Sound (#88)**](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.6056671,-10.1454163): One of the most violent tidal passages on the south coast. Shore diving is restricted to small mainland eddy bays in dead calm conditions during neap slacks.
* [**Barloge Creek / Lough Hyne (#41)**](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.4936676,-9.2938337): The mouth of Lough Hyne produces dramatic tidal rapids (*The Rapids / The Turl*). Barloge Creek itself is sheltered, but entering the narrow neck requires timing with the lough's unique delayed tidal cycle.
* [**Courtmacsherry Pier & Estuary (#25)**](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.6359673,-8.7109165): Tidal flow runs at **2.0 knots off the pontoon/pier** on spring tides, carrying silt out on the ebb.

## 4. Submerged Obstructions & Hazards

eOceanic documents submerged hazards across **58 locations**. For divers, these represent critical safety risks (entanglements, sudden surf breaks) and productive artificial reef habitats.

### 1. Aquaculture & Fish Farming Entanglements (14 Sites)
* Flagged across **Bantry Bay, Coulagh Bay, Dunmanus Bay, and Roaringwater Bay**.
* **Hazard:** Subsurface steel mooring cables, vertical cage lines, lost netting, and high-speed support vessel traffic.
* **Example:** [**Northeast of Great Island (#6)**](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.8796158,-8.2028332) explicitly warns: *"The area beyond is foul, with uncharted and unmarked old aquaculture gear that is revealed only at low water."*

### 2. Drying Reefs, Isolated Pinnacles & Blind Rocks
* **Cotton Rock ([Seven Heads Bay #28](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.5903664,-8.7012167) / [Courtmacsherry #25](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.6359673,-8.7109165)):** Lies 300m offshore; dries to 3.6m and is awash at high water, producing sudden breaking waves and heavy surge.
* **Long Rock ([Sandy Cove #21](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.6780014,-8.5188332)):** A drying rocky reef extending **200m seaward** on the eastern side, creating surge channels and gullies.
* **Cow Rock ([Dunworly Bay #29](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.5837173,-8.7621498)):** Dries 2.6m, surrounded by foul ground and submerged boulders.
* **Sound Rock ([Knockadoon Slip #2](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.8867683,-7.8658333) / [Ballycotton #3](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.8283348,-8.0006666)):** Submerged pinnacles with 2.4m LAT depth, creating dangerous overfalls in swell.

## 5. Underwater Topography & Geological Formations

Topographical data in eOceanic highlights dramatic underwater terrain (walls, pinnacles, gullies, sandbars) ideal for wide-angle underwater photography.

### Vertical Drop-Offs & Submerged Cliffs
* **Old Head of Kinsale ([Holeopen Bay East #22](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.6166649,-8.5333328) & [Holeopen Bay West #23](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.6161499,-8.5504503)):** Bold headlands plunging into sheer underwater drop-offs with deep gullies and dramatic swim-throughs.
* **Big Sovereign ([Oyster Haven #18](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.6953316,-8.4530001) / [Kinsale Harbour #19](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.701683,-8.5175171)):** Two steep rocky outcrops rising 22m and 28m above sea level, extending underwater as near-vertical cliff faces.
* **Barry's Point ([Blindstrand Bay #27](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.6108322,-8.6830835)):** Steep-to rock face offering deep water close to the shore with natural wind protection.

### Submerged Pinnacles & Reef Canals
* **Cork Harbour Entrance Shoal ([Cork City Marina #10](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.898735,-8.4624166)):** Extensive rocky shoal ranging from 10.7m up to a shallow **4.4m rocky pinnacle** near the northeast elbow off Roche's Point.
* **Barloge Creek Canyon ([Barloge Creek #41](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.4936676,-9.2938337)):** Narrow 100m gorge between Bullock Island and the mainland dropping to **12m depth** in the channel before rising abruptly to 3m sand flats inside.
* **Dunworly Bay Gullies ([Dunworly Bay #29](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.5837173,-8.7621498)):** Submerged gullies and boulder fields cutting between exposed rock heads.

### Sandbar & Dune Formations
* [**Youghal (#1)**](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.9519005,-7.8424668) & [**Courtmacsherry (#25)**](https://www.google.com/maps?q=51.6359673,-8.7109165): Shallow sandbars (1.5m LWS) at estuary mouths, creating wide expanses of clean rippled sand.
