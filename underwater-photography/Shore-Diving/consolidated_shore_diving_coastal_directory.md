# Consolidated Ireland Shore Diving & Coastal Directory

This document integrates three primary maritime datasets into a single operational reference for shore diving, coastal reconnaissance, and safety planning:

1. **Cork Shore Diving Catalog** (Personal reconnaissance list exported via Google Maps, 56 sites).
2. **eOceanic Pilotage & Haven Records** (Nautical shelter, drying restrictions, bottom substrate, and hazard profiling, 91 locations).
3. **Diving Ireland (CFT / Comhairle Fo-Thuinn) Launch Infrastructure** (Primary dive hubs with emergency action plans and alternative slipway inventory, 287 nationwide points).

## 4. Operational Shore Diving Rules & Environmental Planning

### 1. Swell & Exposure Rules
* **Atlantic Groundswells (SW / W):** Sites along open headlands (Seven Heads Pier, Dunworly Bay, Sheep Cove, Garnish Pier) experience heavy swell, surge, and reduced visibility during standard North Atlantic weather patterns.
* **Prevailing Wind Fallbacks:**
  * In SW/W blows: Use **Dirk Bay** (sheltered by Galley Head), **Roberts Cove**, **Castlehaven Strand**, or **Adrigole Pier**.
  * In E/SE blows: Switch to the western side of headlands, including **Holeopen Bay West** or inner **Dunmanus Bay** (Canty's Cove, Ahakista).
  * In heavy storms: Restrict diving to 5★ completely protected havens (**Oysterhaven**, **Audley Cove**, **Zetland Pier**, **Snave Pier**, or **Adrigole Pier**).

### 2. Tidal Stream & Overfall Management
* Headland waters off **Seven Heads**, **Old Head of Kinsale**, **Dursey Sound**, and the **Rabbit Island Channel** produce tidal streams exceeding $2.0 \text{ to } 3.0\text{ knots}$ and violent overfalls when current opposes wind.
* Dive outer headland walls strictly at **High Water Slack** or **Low Water Slack**, planning an in-water runtime not exceeding 40 minutes around the turn of the tide.

### 3. Drying Basins & Silt Thresholds
* Estuaries and inner bays marked with $0.0\text{ m LWS}$ (such as **Rosscarbery Pier**, **Aghada**, and **Skeaghanore West**) dry to mud or sand at low water springs.
* Diving these locations at Low Water results in long, exhausting mud walks and immediate zero-visibility conditions from disturbed sediment. Always schedule entries **$1.5 \text{ to } 2\text{ hours before High Water}$**.
