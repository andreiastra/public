## Direct citations of your records

GBIF's literature index also stores the occurrence keys that a paper cites explicitly (as a gbif.org/occurrence URL or ID). Across the whole index, papers cite **1,335** distinct occurrence keys. **None** of your 297 records is among them.

This is rare for iNaturalist records. Museum specimens are routinely cited by occurrence ID, but iNaturalist observations are mostly used through bulk downloads, which link to the dataset or the download DOI, or are cited by their inaturalist.org URL, which GBIF does not read as an occurrence key. The search filter `/literature/search?gbifOccurrenceKey=` fails on the API, so this check reads the facet over all cited keys instead.

## How to read the rest of this report

- **Why the dataset, not your records:** your records on GBIF are not a dataset of their own. iNaturalist publishes all research-grade observations from every contributor as one dataset, and GBIF attaches most citations to a dataset and to the taxa a paper mentions. The dataset-wide figures below therefore count everyone's records.
- **How your species are matched:** the GBIF taxon keys on your records are compared with the taxa GBIF found in each linked paper. A match means the paper mentions a species you recorded.
- **What it cannot show:** whether your own records were used, unless they appear above. A paper on a species you recorded may have used only other people's records of it. The paper's methods or its GBIF download DOI are the only way to confirm.
- **Stronger signals:** a paper on one of your species that also covers a country or period you recorded in is more likely to have used your records.
