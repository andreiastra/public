## Filters applied

1. Inside the polygon around Lough Hyne (51°30'10.5"N 9°18'10.4"W): `geometry=POLYGON((-9.29748 51.51075,-9.3163 51.50589,-9.30232 51.48843,-9.28454 51.49507,-9.29748 51.51075))`
2. Occurrence is present, not an absence record: `occurrenceStatus=PRESENT`
3. The record has a photo: `mediaType=StillImage`
4. Identified to species: has a `speciesKey`
5. Marine species, leaving out Aves and Magnoliopsida (seabirds and coastal plants): a GBIF species profile (WoRMS or Catalogue of Life) marks it as marine
6. No copies: a record from another dataset (such as Explore Your Shore) is left out when an iNaturalist record has the same species, recorder and date, because it republishes the same photo

Filters 1 to 3 can be repeated on [gbif.org/occurrence/search](https://www.gbif.org/occurrence/search) (Location, Geometry; Occurrence status, Present; Media type, Still image). The website can't filter by marine species, so filters 4 to 6 are applied by the script.

The marine flag comes from taxon profiles, so a marine species seen on the shore or from a boat counts too, and a freshwater or terrestrial species never appears. A species with no marine value in its GBIF profile (some seaweeds) is left out. The common name is the first English name GBIF holds for the species; the scientific name is shown in italics where there is none. Species are grouped by their GBIF classification, with the number of species in brackets. There is one thumbnail per record, newest first, and each opens its occurrence on GBIF. Records carry a coordinate uncertainty, so some may come from the surroundings of the lough.
